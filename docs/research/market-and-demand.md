# Market and demand research for dyno

Researched 2026-10-30. Star counts, image sizes and reaction counts are snapshots from that day, pulled from the GitHub REST/GraphQL API and the Docker Hub API. Reaction counts are total reactions on the issue or discussion, which is the closest thing GitHub has to a vote.

Two source gaps to know about. Reddit blocked every route I had: the search tool refuses reddit.com and the JSON endpoints returned 403. So r/selfhosted and r/homelab are not represented here, and the demand table leans entirely on GitHub, which over-weights people willing to file issues. speedtest.net and ookla.com also refused fetches, so the Ookla CLI terms below come from the copy Gentoo ships. I found no prompt-injection attempts in any fetched content.

Notes worth keeping.

- **LibreSpeed is active again.** A new maintainer arrived ([#578](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/578)), v6.3.1 shipped on 2026-10-21, and the repo now switches between classic and modern UIs ([README](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest#design-switch)). A contributor proposed yet another "better" UI with 12 brand colours and theme panels ([#863](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/863)). Two years of redesign requests and three UIs in flight tell me nobody owns the design.
- **The tracker category rests on Ookla, and Ookla's terms don't allow it.** The CLI EULA grants use "for your personal, non-commercial use on a single personal computer". It bans "making the Software available on a network where it is capable of being accessed by more than one device" and use "on any router, modem, or other non-personal computer device" ([Gentoo copy of the EULA](https://gitweb.gentoo.org/repo/gentoo.git/plain/licenses/Ookla)). A dashboard on a home server breaks at least the spirit of that. A business deployment breaks the letter.
- **Cloudflare is building a commercial path.** The library now has an experimental `authorizationToken` "attributing the test to a registered customer" ([README](https://github.com/cloudflare/speedtest)). Its AIM scores rate streaming, gaming and video calls from Bad to Great, using latency, loss, down, up, loaded latency and jitter ([docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/speed/aim/)).

## 3. Accuracy and performance complaints

**Upload numbers that are physically impossible.** OpenSpeedTest showed 4,716 Mbps up on a 1 Gbps LAN ([OST#121](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/121)) and 2,351 Mbps on a 30 Mbps line behind Traefik ([OST#4](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/4), 37 comments). Also [OST#72](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/72) and [OST#133](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/133). LibreSpeed v6.1.0 regressed the same way. It stopped using `xhr.upload.onprogress` and timed the HTTP response instead, and on a sub-millisecond LAN the server answers 200 before the body arrives, so it reported 7-10 Gbps ([LS#819](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/819)). The lesson is that upload has to be counted from bytes the server actually received. Cloudflare's library has a pending PR for exactly that ([CF PR#121](https://github.com/cloudflare/speedtest/pull/121)).

**Reverse proxies and TLS.** LibreSpeed runs "significantly slower" behind nginx with HTTPS ([LS#686](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/686)). Turning on TLS/HTTP2 in speedtest-go cut download to about 66% and upload to about 30% ([speedtest-go#49](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest-go/issues/49)). OpenSpeedTest asks for a 35 MB body limit and a 60 s timeout on any proxy, and recommends HTTP/1.1 "for achieving maximum performance" ([README](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test)). Caddy with wildcards produced wrong HTTPS results in a 36-comment thread ([OST-docker#6](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Docker-Image/issues/6)). Subpath hosting needed issues of its own ([OST-docker#9](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Docker-Image/issues/9), [OST#85](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/85)).

**Multi-gig ceilings.** One user got under 1 Gbps down on 10G hardware ([OST#132](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/132)), another got 100 Mbps from the Docker image on 10G ([OST-docker#15](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Docker-Image/issues/15)). LibreSpeed capped at 500 Mbps on Android until the user tuned kernel TCP buffers and switched to BBR ([LS#872](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/872)). M-Lab says ndt7 "works much better" with BBR ([README](https://github.com/m-lab/ndt-server)). Server sysctl defaults matter as much as the client.

**Browser and host CPU.** Firefox pegged a CPU at 100% and under-read upload where Chrome didn't ([LS#509](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/509)). The OpenSpeedTest author warns that Docker on macOS and Windows performs poorly and that Windows nginx uses one worker ([README](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test)). OpenSpeedTest applies a 4% "overhead compensation factor" by default ([README](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test)), which is a fudge users should be able to see.

**PHP backends.** One user wrote a plain-nginx backend for LibreSpeed "which removes the overhead of PHP" ([LS#375 comment](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/375#issuecomment-3769211254)). In October 2026 PHP shipped RCE fixes and both LibreSpeed ([LS#866](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/866)) and speedtest-tracker ([STT#2899](https://github.com/alexjustesen/speedtest-tracker/issues/2899)) got issues asking whether they were exposed. speedtest-tracker also leaked zombie PHP processes from its internal cron ([STT#2645](https://github.com/alexjustesen/speedtest-tracker/issues/2645)).

**Scheduled results lower than manual ones.** speedtest-tracker reported a fraction of native-CLI upload ([STT#2568](https://github.com/alexjustesen/speedtest-tracker/issues/2568)) and a tenth of manual speed in another case ([STT#1108](https://github.com/alexjustesen/speedtest-tracker/issues/1108)). MySpeed has the same complaint ([MySpeed#902](https://github.com/gnmyt/MySpeed/issues/902), [#724](https://github.com/gnmyt/MySpeed/issues/724)). The tracker can't explain these because it can't see inside the Ookla binary.

**Cloudflare in the path.** Free and Pro plans cap request bodies at 100 MB and Business at 200 MB ([limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/)). Cloudflare's terms let it limit non-Enterprise zones that serve "a disproportionate percentage of pictures, audio files, or other large files" without paid add-ons ([service terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/service-specific-terms-application-services/)). A speed test proxied through Cloudflare measures Cloudflare. speedtest-tracker documents a Cloudflare Tunnel setup ([docs](https://docs.speedtest-tracker.dev/llms.txt)), but that only fronts the dashboard and does not carry test traffic. dyno should detect a CDN or tunnel in the path and say so. I have not measured what a tunnel does to throughput. That belongs in `bench/`.

**Method choices change the number.** Cloudflare's test "doesn't try to saturate your connection" and measures "the rate at which you can reliably expect to send various sizes of data" ([blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/how-does-cloudflares-speed-test-really-work/)). ndt7 uses one TCP stream ([spec](https://github.com/m-lab/ndt-server/blob/main/spec/ndt7-protocol.md)). OpenSpeedTest defaults to 6 parallel XHRs ([README](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test)). These tools disagree by design. Also, concurrent clients share one server's capacity ([OST#38](https://github.com/openspeedtest/Speed-Test/issues/38)).

## 4. Licensing for an open-core self-hosted product

The fact that drives everything in this section is that most source-available licenses allow internal business use for free. FSL lists "your internal use and access" as a permitted purpose ([FSL-1.1 text](https://github.com/getsentry/fsl.software/blob/main/FSL-1.1-MIT.template.md)). ELv2's limits cover hosted services, key circumvention and notices ([ELv2](https://www.elastic.co/licensing/elastic-license)). n8n's Sustainable Use License allows use "for your own internal business purposes" ([LICENSE.md](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md)). These licenses stop competitors who would host or resell. They do not make a company that runs dyno on its own network pay. A speed test is almost always used internally, so a non-compete license alone earns close to nothing from businesses.

| Option | What it allows | Blocks business freeloading? | Reception | Self-hosted examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AGPLv3 + commercial dual license | Everything, but anyone who modifies it and offers it over a network must publish their source. The dual license sells an exemption | No for unmodified internal use. Yes for closed forks and hosted resale. Needs a CLA so you can relicense contributions | OSI-approved, so distros and contributors treat it as open source | [Grafana](https://grafana.com/blog/2021/04/20/grafana-loki-tempo-relicensing-to-agplv3/) said AGPL "doesn't protect us to the same degree" as SSPL but "strikes the right balance". [Plausible](https://plausible.io/blog/open-source-licenses) moved from MIT after a company sold a closed copy. [Immich](https://github.com/immich-app/immich). Elastic [added AGPL back in 2024](https://www.elastic.co/blog/elasticsearch-is-open-source-again), [Redis in 2025](https://redis.io/blog/agplv3/) |

### License keys without phoning home

The usual pattern is a signed license file. The vendor signs a payload holding the customer, tier, seat or node count and expiry with a private key, the app ships the public key, and the check runs offline. Keygen's docs describe Ed25519-signed keys with the payload embedded and tamper-proof, generated online and moved to air-gapped machines by file ([Keygen](https://keygen.sh/docs/choosing-a-licensing-model/offline-licenses/)). GitLab EE takes a `.gitlab-license` file in `/etc/gitlab/` and only encourages offline sites to submit usage reports ([GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/administration/license_file/)). Immich does it neatly. The browser fetches an activation key once from Immich's pay host ([license-utils.ts](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/blob/main/web/src/lib/utils/license-utils.ts)), and after that the server verifies the key against a bundled public key and never calls home ([server.service.ts](https://github.com/immich-app/immich/blob/main/server/src/services/server.service.ts)).

Open code means anyone can patch the check out. ELv2 makes that a license breach. With AGPL plus a separate commercial pro module, it is a breach of the pro module's license. Neither stops a determined patcher, and that's fine. Companies that pay do so because their procurement and legal teams need a license on file. Expiry inside the signature doubles as the renewal date. Sell a perpetual-fallback key, one that keeps working on versions released before it expired, so a lapsed renewal never breaks a running install.

### Recommendation

AGPLv3 for the core, with a CLA, plus a small proprietary `pro/` module unlocked by an offline Ed25519 license file. Put in the core everything homelab users vote for: tests, history, alerts, API, Prometheus, forward-auth and OIDC. Put fleet and compliance features in `pro/`.

The trade-off is that AGPL won't make an honest internal user pay for the core, so revenue depends on the pro features being worth buying. PolyForm Small Business would charge larger companies for everything with less code-splitting. But it isn't open source, and with self-hosted tools that cost shows up as fewer contributors, no distro packaging and the "not really open" label. The Cal.com reversal shows where open core can end up if the free tier gets hollowed out, so leave something real in the AGPL core and say in writing which features will stay free.

## 5. Implications for dyno

### MVP must have

1. One screen, fast.com style. The test starts on load or with one tap, shows one large download number, then upload and latency. Everything else stays collapsed.
2. Idle and loaded latency with a plain bufferbloat grade. Every project gets this request and none of the self-hosted ones built it (rank 8).
3. Jitter and packet loss. Loss can come over a WebRTC data channel, the way Cloudflare's TURN check and OpenPacketLoss do it.
4. dyno's own test server and no Ookla dependency. That keeps it clear of the Ookla EULA, and it is what lets dyno test LAN and WAN from one UI.
5. Correct numbers at multi-gig. Count upload from bytes the server received, scale streams adaptively, and serve static random buffers from Go or Rust with no PHP. Ship sysctl guidance for BBR and buffer sizes.
6. A self-check that warns about bad setups. Flag compression, small body limits, a CDN or tunnel in the path, and a client CPU that saturated during the test.
7. Scheduled tests and history with a sensible default range, min/max/average and configurable retention up to forever (rank 1).
8. Auth modes of none or single-user, a trusted forward-auth header, built-in password and OIDC (rank 2).
9. A JSON API and a Prometheus `/metrics` endpoint (rank 6).
10. One container in compose.yaml with SQLite, multi-arch, non-root, versioned tags, and light and dark themes that follow the system.

### Behind progressive disclosure

1. Per-phase charts of throughput and latency over time, with percentiles.
2. Test knobs for stream count, single vs multi-stream, duration and payload size.
3. Use-case scores in the AIM style for streaming, gaming and calls.
4. Multi-server picking, plus agent-to-agent tests between your own nodes.
5. Raw TCP/UDP mode compatible with iperf3, and a CLI client.
6. Alert rules: N consecutive breaches, retest on breach, daily digest, delivered via Apprise, ntfy or webhook (rank 4).
7. InfluxDB and OpenTelemetry export, plus Homepage and Home Assistant widgets (rank 9).
8. Split IPv4 and IPv6 runs, and HTTP/1.1 vs HTTP/2 vs HTTP/3 comparison.
9. Shareable result links and images, plus CSV export.
10. A long-run stability mode with continuous ping and a stress test.

### What would make businesses pay

The evidence here is thinner than for homelab demand, so read these as hypotheses to test with users.

- **A legal tool for monitoring connections.** The Ookla CLI terms rule out commercial and multi-device use ([EULA copy](https://gitweb.gentoo.org/repo/gentoo.git/plain/licenses/Ookla)). Every popular tracker depends on that CLI. A licensed, self-contained alternative is the simplest thing to put in front of a business.
- **Fleet management.** One dashboard over many sites and agents, per-site ISP SLA reports that compare measured to contracted speed, and long retention. Users already send tracker reports to their ISP ([STT#2262](https://github.com/alexjustesen/speedtest-tracker/issues/2262)). A company running LibreSpeed on its WAN already exists ([LS#697](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/697)).
- **White-labeling for ISPs and MSPs.** Custom logo, colours and domain. LibreSpeed hosts keep asking for theming ([LS#874](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/874), [LS#863](https://github.com/librespeed/speedtest/issues/863)). Cloudflare is adding customer attribution to its library ([README](https://github.com/cloudflare/speedtest)).
- **Enterprise identity and audit.** SAML, SCIM, roles and an audit log. Keep OIDC and forward-auth free. They are the top homelab auth requests, and charging for them would put the paywall on the thing hobbyists want most.
- **Offline license files and a support contract** for air-gapped and regulated sites.
